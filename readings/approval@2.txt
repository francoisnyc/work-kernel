approval@2

Key
- a customer: a person outside the organisation.
- an agent: a program acting for a person; it may do only what both it and that person may do, and a field that names a person never names an agent.
- the kernel: the system that runs the rules of every Type; it is never a person.
- a reaction: a write a Type makes on another Item when something happens to one of its own; the other Item's Type must allow it.

Fields
- `f_title` (Request): text
- `f_approver` (Approver): a person
- `f_delegate` (Hand over to): a person

In short
1. An approval starts in pending.
2. It moves only as listed below; any other move is refused.
3. Every write keeps this true: `f_approver` is set; a write that would not is refused (approver_missing).
4. Every write keeps this true: `f_delegate` is unset or `f_delegate` is `f_approver`; a write that would not is refused (delegate_not_approver).

Who writes what
5. Only the kernel writes `f_title`: access_request's reaction `request_approvals`.
6. Only the kernel writes `f_approver`: access_request's reaction `request_approvals`, and the move from pending to pending.
7. No two approvals in one container, both in pending or approved, hold the same `f_approver`.
8. No agent writes its fields.

Waiting for a decision (`pending`, active)
9. While it is in pending, it waits on whoever `f_approver` names, or anyone with the role access_admin.
10. Approve: whoever `f_approver` names, but never anyone who took part in creating its container, moves it from pending to approved, only if its container's state is pending.
11. Reject: whoever `f_approver` names, but never anyone who took part in creating its container, moves it from pending to rejected, only if its container's state is pending.
12. Hand over: whoever `f_approver` names, or anyone with the role access_admin, but never anyone who took part in creating its container, moves it from pending to pending, once `f_delegate` is set, only if its container's state is pending and `f_delegate` is not `f_approver`; the move sets `f_approver` to `f_delegate`.
13. Only a reaction moves it from pending to canceled.

Approved (`approved`, done)
14. On entering approved, `move_approved` moves its container from pending to approved; if its container's rules refuse the move, it is skipped.
15. Nothing leaves approved.

Rejected (`rejected`, done)
16. On entering rejected, `move_rejected` moves its container from pending to rejected; if its container's rules refuse the move, it is skipped.
17. Nothing leaves rejected.

Closed (`canceled`, canceled)
18. Nothing leaves canceled.

What other Types may do to it
19. access_request's reaction `close_on_approved` may move it to canceled.
20. access_request's reaction `close_on_rejected` may move it to canceled.
21. access_request's reaction `close_on_withdrawn` may move it to canceled.
22. access_request's reaction `request_approvals` may create it, setting `f_approver` and `f_title`.

Who sees it
23. A customer who sees it sees none of its fields, only its header.
24. No one moves it to another container or narrows who sees it.

Not covered by this reading
- Who can see a given approval: that is set for each Item and its container, not by its Type.
- Who may create an approval, and where, and who may move or narrow one, once a later version exists: the latest version of approval decides, for every version.
- How the kernel keeps its promises (retries, each write made once, recovery after a failure): the same for every Type.
