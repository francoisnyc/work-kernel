incident@4

Key
- the requester: the person an Item is for: whoever created it, or the person they created it for.
- staff: people in the organisation, and agents acting for them; a customer is never staff.
- a customer: a person outside the organisation.
- an agent: a program acting for a person; it may do only what both it and that person may do, and a field that names a person never names an agent.
- the kernel: the system that runs the rules of every Type; it is never a person.
- the timer: the kernel's clock; it makes only the moves a Type gives it.
- a reaction: a write a Type makes on another Item when something happens to one of its own; the other Item's Type must allow it.

Fields
- `f_title`: text
- `f_priority`: one of P1, P2, P3 or P4
- `f_impact`: one of extensive, significant, moderate or minor
- `f_assignee`: a person
- `f_resolution`: one of fixed, workaround, wontfix or duplicate
- `f_due_first_response`: an instant
- `f_due_resolution`: an instant

In short
1. An incident starts in new.
2. It moves only as listed below; any other move is refused.
3. Every write keeps this true: `f_priority` is set; a write that would not is refused (priority_missing).

Who starts it
4. An incident is created only inside a desk.
5. Any staff member who can see the container creates one; a customer creates one only where a container lets them.
6. While this is the latest version of incident, these rules apply to creating an incident of any version.

Who writes what
7. Only the kernel writes `f_due_first_response`: sla_goal's reactions `cancel_first_response`, `clear_first_response`, `hide_first_response` and `show_first_response`.
8. Only the kernel writes `f_due_resolution`: sla_goal's reactions `cancel_resolution`, `clear_resolution`, `hide_resolution` and `show_resolution`.
9. No agent writes its fields; an agent acting for a customer sets only what the customer may.

Links
10. Anyone who can see it links it to a bug as `caused_by`, any number; a bug sees it as `causes`.

new (to triage)
11. On entering new, `sla_start_first_response` creates a sla_goal@3 inside it, with `f_days_off` = `f_days_off` of its container's `calendar`, `f_metric` = first_response, `f_started_at` = the time of the write, `f_week` = `f_week` of its container's `calendar`, `f_zone` = `f_zone` of its container's `calendar`, and `f_due_at` and `f_target_s` computed from `f_priority`, the time of the write, its container's `calendar` and its container's `sla_policy`.
12. On entering new, `sla_start_resolution` creates a sla_goal@3 inside it, with `f_days_off` = `f_days_off` of its container's `calendar`, `f_metric` = resolution, `f_started_at` = the time of the write, `f_week` = `f_week` of its container's `calendar`, `f_zone` = `f_zone` of its container's `calendar`, and `f_due_at` and `f_target_s` computed from `f_priority`, the time of the write, its container's `calendar` and its container's `sla_policy`.
13. While it is in new, it waits on anyone with the role desk_agent.
14. Any staff member who can see it moves it from new to in_progress.
15. The requester, or anyone with the role desk_agent, moves it from new to canceled.

in_progress (active)
16. On entering in_progress from resolved, `sla_restart_resolution` creates a sla_goal@3 inside it, with `f_days_off` = `f_days_off` of its container's `calendar`, `f_metric` = resolution, `f_started_at` = the time of the write, `f_week` = `f_week` of its container's `calendar`, `f_zone` = `f_zone` of its container's `calendar`, and `f_due_at` and `f_target_s` computed from `f_priority`, the time of the write, its container's `calendar` and its container's `sla_policy`.
17. On entering in_progress from waiting_for_customer, `sla_resume` moves the sla_goals inside it whose `f_metric` is resolution from paused to running.
18. Any staff member who can see it moves it from in_progress to waiting_for_customer.
19. Any staff member who can see it moves it from in_progress to resolved, once `f_resolution` is set.
20. Any staff member who can see it moves it from in_progress to canceled.

waiting_for_customer (waiting)
21. On entering waiting_for_customer, `sla_pause` moves the sla_goals inside it whose `f_metric` is resolution from running to paused.
22. Any staff member who can see it moves it from waiting_for_customer to in_progress.
23. Any staff member who can see it moves it from waiting_for_customer to resolved, once `f_resolution` is set.

resolved (done)
24. On entering resolved, `sla_stop` moves the sla_goals inside it from running or paused to completed.
25. 120 hours after it enters resolved, if it is still there, the timer moves it to closed.
26. Only the timer moves it from resolved to closed.
27. The requester, or anyone with the role desk_agent, moves it from resolved to in_progress; the move clears `f_resolution`.

closed (done)
28. Nothing leaves closed.

canceled (canceled)
29. On entering canceled, `sla_cancel` moves the sla_goals inside it from running or paused to canceled.
30. Nothing leaves canceled.

When something is created inside it
31. When a person other than the requester creates a reply inside it, `sla_answered` moves the sla_goals inside it whose `f_metric` is first_response from running to completed.
32. When the requester creates a reply inside it while it is in waiting_for_customer, `customer_replied` moves it to in_progress.

What other Types may do to it
33. sla_goal's reaction `cancel_first_response` may set `f_due_first_response`.
34. sla_goal's reaction `cancel_resolution` may set `f_due_resolution`.
35. sla_goal's reaction `clear_first_response` may set `f_due_first_response`.
36. sla_goal's reaction `clear_resolution` may set `f_due_resolution`.
37. sla_goal's reaction `hide_first_response` may set `f_due_first_response`.
38. sla_goal's reaction `hide_resolution` may set `f_due_resolution`.
39. sla_goal's reaction `show_first_response` may set `f_due_first_response`.
40. sla_goal's reaction `show_resolution` may set `f_due_resolution`.
41. Items inside it may read `f_assignee` as `breach_contact`.

Who sees it
42. A customer who sees it sees only `f_title`, `f_priority`, `f_impact` and `f_resolution`, and sets only those when creating it.
43. The customer who requested it sees every reply inside it, and may create one there.
44. Only holders of desk_agent move it to another container or narrow who sees it.

Not covered by this reading
- Who can see a given incident: that is set for each Item and its container, not by its Type.
- Who may create an incident, and where, and who may move or narrow one, once a later version exists: the latest version of incident decides, for every version.
- How the kernel keeps its promises (retries, each write made once, recovery after a failure): the same for every Type.
